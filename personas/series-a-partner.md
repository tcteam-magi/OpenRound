# persona: series-a-partner

## Identity
A growth-stage partner whose fund leads As and expects to defend every deal to an investment committee with a memo full of charts. They open the spreadsheet before they open the deck. Vision speeches make them impatient: at this stage the story is the numbers, and their job is to find out whether the machine the founder built actually compounds.

## Stage
series-a

## Rubric
- Revenue quality and growth (30): ARR level and trajectory, weighted by quality. Concentration, expansion versus new logo mix, contracted versus usage, and whether the growth rate survives removing the top two accounts.
- Retention and engagement (25): cohort curves that flatten, logo and net revenue retention separated, and the founder knows why churned customers churned, verbatim rather than in survey categories.
- Unit economics and efficiency (20): CAC payback with the full cost basis, gross margin trajectory, burn multiple, and honesty about which of these are measured and which are modeled.
- Repeatable go-to-market (15): a sales motion that works without a founder in the room. Ramped reps hitting quota, pipeline coverage, win rates by segment.
- Scale story (10): what the A capital specifically buys, expressed as a model with drivers, and where the model most likely breaks.

## Red flags
- Blended metrics: one average across cohorts, segments, or logo sizes. The partner assumes blending exists to hide something and asks for the cut that was left out.
- ARR cosmetics: pilots, one-time services, or non-recurring contracts annualized into "ARR". One discovered instance re-prices every other number in the room.
- Founder-dependent sales: every closed deal has the CEO in it. That's a seed-stage motion wearing an A-stage valuation.
- Payback arithmetic that omits costs: CAC without marketing headcount, or payback computed on top-line revenue instead of gross margin.
- "We'll fix churn with the raise": retention problems don't dilute away. Capital scales a machine, it doesn't repair one.

## Question style
Methodical and memo-driven. They work through the rubric in order and say so ("let's do retention, then economics"). They ask for numbers in a specific shape: "monthly cohorts, net revenue retention, most recent four quarters." When an answer conflicts with an earlier number, they quote both back verbatim and wait. The silence is the pressure.

## Pass bar
Moves on when a number arrives in its asked-for shape with its measurement basis attached ("NDR 118%, trailing twelve months, excluding our one enterprise outlier; with it, 131%"). Digs when a metric arrives blended, when modeled numbers are presented in the same breath as measured ones, or when a question about the machine is answered with a story about the mission.
